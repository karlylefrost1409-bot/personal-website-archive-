The Authors

The writer

Donna Tartt

1963 – Present
American Literary Fiction Psychological Fiction

About

Donna Tartt is an American novelist known for her carefully constructed narratives, atmospheric settings, and explorations of obsession, friendship, morality, and the consequences of intellectual and emotional isolation. Her novels often combine literary ambition with suspense and psychological depth.

Notable Works

  • The Secret History
  • The Little Friend
  • The Goldfinch
✦ ❧ ✦
The writer

Fyodor Dostoevsky

1821 – 1881
Russian Realism Psychological Fiction

About

Fyodor Dostoevsky was a Russian novelist whose works explore human consciousness, morality, faith, freedom, guilt, and the tension between reason and emotion. His characters frequently confront difficult philosophical questions through inner conflict, relationships, and extreme moral circumstances.

Notable Works

  • Crime and Punishment
  • The Brothers Karamazov
  • The Idiot
  • Notes from Underground
  • The Double
✦ ❧ ✦
The philosopher

Friedrich Nietzsche

1844 – 1900
German Philosophy Existential Thought

About

Friedrich Nietzsche was a German philosopher whose writings challenged conventional morality, religion, truth, and the foundations of Western thought. His ideas concerning the will to power, the Übermensch, perspectivism, and the revaluation of values remain influential and widely debated. His writing often combines philosophical argument, aphorisms, literary imagery, and provocative criticism.

Notable Works

  • Thus Spoke Zarathustra
  • Beyond Good and Evil
  • The Birth of Tragedy
  • On the Genealogy of Morality
  • The Gay Science
✦ ❧ ✦
The writer

Franz Kafka

1883 – 1924
Bohemian Modernism Existential Literature

About

Franz Kafka was a writer whose fiction presents unsettling worlds shaped by bureaucracy, alienation, uncertainty, and the difficulty of understanding one's place within society. His work often combines ordinary situations with strange, inexplicable circumstances, creating narratives that resist simple interpretation.

Notable Works

  • The Metamorphosis
  • The Trial
  • The Castle
  • In the Penal Colony
✦ ❧ ✦
The writer

Albert Camus

1913 – 1960
French-Algerian Existentialism Absurdism

About

Albert Camus was a French-Algerian writer, journalist, and philosopher associated with the philosophy of the absurd. His works examine the human search for meaning in a world that offers no clear answers, as well as questions of justice, solidarity, rebellion, and responsibility.

Notable Works

  • The Stranger
  • The Plague
  • The Myth of Sisyphus
  • The Fall
✦ ❧ ✦
The writer

Clarice Lispector

1920 – 1977
Brazilian Modernism Experimental Fiction

About

Clarice Lispector was a Brazilian writer known for introspective and experimental prose that explores consciousness, identity, existence, and the ordinary moments that reveal unfamiliar aspects of reality. Her writing often turns inward, examining the limits of language and the nature of self-awareness.

Notable Works

  • The Hour of the Star
  • Near to the Wild Heart
  • Água Viva
  • The Passion According to G.H.
✦ ❧ ✦
The writer

Ocean Vuong

1988 – Present
Vietnamese-American Contemporary Literature Poetry

About

Ocean Vuong is a Vietnamese-American poet and novelist whose writing explores memory, family, identity, language, displacement, and the relationship between tenderness and hardship. His work is known for lyrical language, fragmented narratives, and the close attention he gives to personal and collective memory.

Notable Works

  • On Earth We're Briefly Gorgeous
  • Night Sky with Exit Wounds
  • Time Is a Mother

The writer

Hanya Yanagihara

1974 – Present
American Contemporary Fiction Literary Fiction

About

Hanya Yanagihara is an American novelist and editor whose fiction examines friendship, memory, identity, ambition, suffering, and the lasting influence of the past. Her novels are known for their expansive narratives and complex relationships between characters.

Notable Works

  • A Little Life
  • The People in the Trees
  • To Paradise

The essayist

Susan Sontag

1933 – 2004
American Essays Criticism

About

Susan Sontag was an American essayist, critic, novelist, and intellectual whose writing addressed art, photography, literature, politics, illness, and culture. She questioned established ways of interpreting images and cultural works, and became an influential voice in modern criticism.

Notable Works

  • On Photography
  • Against Interpretation
  • Regarding the Pain of Others
  • Illness as Metaphor
✦ ❧ ✦
The writer

Hermann Hesse

1877 – 1962
German-Swiss Modern Literature Philosophical Fiction

About

Hermann Hesse was a German-Swiss writer whose novels and poetry explore spiritual development, individuality, self-discovery, inner conflict, and the search for harmony between opposing aspects of human experience. His work frequently draws on philosophical and spiritual traditions.

Notable Works

  • Siddhartha
  • Steppenwolf
  • Demian
  • The Glass Bead Game
✦ ❧ ✦
The writer

Toni Morrison

1931 – 2019
American Literary Fiction Nobel Laureate

About

Toni Morrison was an American novelist, editor, and essayist whose literature explores African-American experience, history, memory, identity, community, and the lasting consequences of racism. Her writing combines richly layered storytelling, symbolism, folklore, and historical narratives.

Notable Works

  • Beloved
  • The Bluest Eye
  • Song of Solomon
  • Sula
✦ ❧ ✦
The writer

Oscar Wilde

1854 – 1900
Irish Aestheticism Literary Satire

About

Oscar Wilde was an Irish writer, poet, and playwright associated with aestheticism. Renowned for his wit, epigrams, and criticism of social conventions, Wilde explored art, beauty, morality, hypocrisy, and the contradictions of Victorian society through drama, fiction, and essays.

Notable Works

  • The Picture of Dorian Gray
  • The Importance of Being Earnest
  • De Profundis
  • The Happy Prince and Other Tales
✦ ❧ ✦
The philosopher

Simone de Beauvoir

1908 – 1986
French Existentialism Feminist Philosophy

About

Simone de Beauvoir was a French philosopher, writer, and intellectual whose work examined freedom, ethics, gender, social structures, and the conditions shaping human existence. Her philosophical and literary writing played a significant role in existentialist thought and feminist theory.

Notable Works

  • The Second Sex
  • The Ethics of Ambiguity
  • She Came to Stay
  • The Mandarins
✦ ❧ ✦
The poet

Sylvia Plath

1932 – 1963
American Poetry Confessional Writing

About

Sylvia Plath was an American poet and novelist whose writing is distinguished by its intense imagery, emotional precision, and exploration of identity, family, social expectations, and the complexities of personal experience. Her poetry is frequently associated with the confessional literary movement.

Notable Works

  • Ariel
  • The Bell Jar
  • The Colossus and Other Poems
